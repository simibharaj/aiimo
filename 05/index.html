how the points work

you and one other player each pick a color in secret. green is cooperate. pink is defect.

say they pick green. what do you pick?

pink pays you more both times. against green it's 5 instead of 3, and against pink it's 1 instead of 0. the other player sees the same points. if you both go pink, you each get 1 when you both could have had 3. that's the prisoner's dilemma.

how do you win without beating anyone?

robert axelrod gave people an odd assignment. write a program that decides whether to cooperate or take advantage of the other player. once the match starts, your code makes every decision for you. and whoever’s across from you gets 200 chances to respond.

fourteen entries came in. anatol rapoport’s was called tit for tat. it gave everyone the benefit of the doubt on the first move. after that, it did whatever you’d just done. take advantage of it on tuesday and it would push back on wednesday. cooperate on wednesday and by thursday you could be working together again.

here’s the part i love. rapoport’s program never finished a match ahead of its opponent. how did it come first? axelrod added up points. two programs that kept cooperating earned three points each, round after round. grab five points once and spend the next hundred rounds trading blows, and suddenly five looks pretty cheap.
